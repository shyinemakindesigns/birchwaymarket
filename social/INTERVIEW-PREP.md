# Interview prep: owning the Birchway case study

Live site: https://birchway-casestudy.netlify.app

The Strategy and Copy and search chapters were drafted with Claude Code from the brief, and the Reflection page says so. Before an interview, read each chapter, change anything you would not defend, and practise the answers below in your own words. The honest framing is strong: you directed the work, set the criteria, reviewed every output and can explain every decision.

## The one-line pitch

"I made the production the deliverable: one approved holiday creative, adapted to eight specs, checked by a QA list that measures the actual files, filed and tracked like a real job, with the strategy and copy that support it."

## Questions you will likely get

**Walk me through the project.**
Brief comes in as a ticket. Spec sheet and folder structure are locked first. Then the adaptations, two review rounds, QA, delivery. Point to the gallery, then the QA page's v1 failure (logo 4 px from the edge, CTA at 1.78:1) and the v2 fix.

**What did you do and what did AI do?**
You: creative direction, which specs apply, QA criteria and thresholds, naming and versioning, board stages and priorities, and review and sign-off. Design tools: Canva for size research, Illustrator for vectors, Figma for layout and motion. AI: concept routes (Claude Design) and the production build in code (Claude Code), from your specs. Say it plainly; the Reflection page backs you up.

**Why these audience segments?** (Strategy)
They split the brief's audience by behaviour, not demographics: the host plans weeks out, the contributor decides late, the value planner compares prices. Each needs a different message and platform. Say they are hypotheses, and name how you would test them (loyalty data, a short survey).

**Where is your market research?**
It is a research plan, deliberately. A fictional brand has no data, so the chapter names each hypothesis, the tool that would test it (Google Trends, Keyword Planner, Meta Ad Library, TikTok Creative Center) and the decision it feeds. Inventing numbers would be worse than labelling a plan.

**How would you forecast traffic?**
Budget, divided by CPM, gives impressions. Multiply by CTR for clicks (search uses spend divided by CPC), by an arrival rate for sessions, and by CVR for conversions. The rates on the page are placeholders. In a real job you would use the account's own history, and the A/B tests in weeks one and two would move budget.

**Why does each platform have one job?**
So performance is judged against the right goal. Pinterest builds planning intent and is not judged on sales; DOOH is a near-store reminder measured by brand lift, not clicks.

**How did you write the copy?**
Voice rules first (warm, useful, specific), then each line written to its placement's limit and checked live on the page. Google search headlines max out at 30 characters; Meta headlines truncate after about 27.

**What is the difference between SEO, AEO and GEO?**
SEO: rank pages in search results. AEO: be the direct answer in featured snippets and voice (question headings, answer first in 40 to 60 words). GEO: be cited correctly by AI assistants (consistent facts, original material, structured data, freshness). This site applies all three: prerendered HTML, structured data, sitemap and llms.txt.

**Why tie UTMs to filenames?**
utm_content carries the exact file and version, so analytics reports which v2 won, and the file that gets more budget is the one that was tested.
